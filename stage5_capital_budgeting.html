Stage 5: Capital Budgeting & Investment Decisions

Master project appraisal techniques: Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), Payback Period, and Profitability Index (PI). Know exactly why NPV beats IRR when projects conflict.

1. The Four Capital Budgeting Appraisal Techniques

TechniqueFormula / DefinitionAccept / Reject RuleKey Advantage & Limitation
1. Net Present Value (NPV) $\sum_{t=1}^n \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} - \text{Initial Outlay}$ Accept if $\text{NPV} > 0$
(Adds rupee value to shareholders)		 Gold Standard. Directly measures wealth created; additive across independent projects.
2. Internal Rate of Return (IRR) Discount rate $r$ where $\text{NPV} = 0$ Accept if $\text{IRR} > \text{Cost of Capital } (r)$ Intuitive percentage return; but scale-blind and assumes reinvestment at IRR.
3. Payback Period Years required to recover the initial cash outlay Accept if Payback $\le$ Target cutoff Measures liquidity and speed; fatal flaw: ignores TVM and all cash flows after payback!
4. Profitability Index (PI) $\frac{\text{PV of Cash Inflows}}{\text{Initial Outlay}}$ Accept if $\text{PI} > 1.0$
(Equivalent to $\text{NPV} > 0$)		 Indispensable for ranking projects under Capital Rationing (limited funds).

⚖️ The Big Conflict: NPV vs IRR in Mutually Exclusive Projects Guaranteed Exam Question

When choosing between two mutually exclusive projects (you can only pick one), NPV and IRR may rank them differently.

🚨 The Golden Rule: ALWAYS OBEY NPV!

If Project A has a higher NPV and Project B has a higher IRR, always choose Project A (highest NPV).

Why Does IRR Fail in Conflicts?

  1. The Scale Problem (Scale-Blindness):
    A project with an outlay of ₹10,000 that returns ₹20,000 has an IRR of $100\%$ but generates only ₹10,000 of wealth. A project with an outlay of ₹1 Crore that returns ₹1.25 Crore has an IRR of $25\%$ but generates ₹25,00,000 of wealth! Shareholders care about absolute rupee wealth, not percentage bragging rights.
  2. The Reinvestment Rate Fallacy:
    NPV assumes intermediate cash inflows are reinvested at the firm's realistic Cost of Capital (WACC). IRR assumes cash flows can be reinvested at the IRR itself (e.g., assuming you can reinvest intermediate cash at 40%, which is virtually impossible).
  3. Multiple IRRs:
    If a project has unconventional cash flows (outflows followed by inflows followed by another outflow, such as environmental cleanup at mine closure), there can be multiple internal rates of return or none at all.

3. Capital Rationing & Project Ranking via Profitability Index (PI)

When a firm does not have infinite capital and faces a strict budget ceiling, simply picking projects by highest NPV can lead to suboptimal capital usage. Instead, rank projects by Profitability Index (PI) to get the most rupee value per rupee of scarce budget.

Profitability Index Formula:
PI = PV of Cash Inflows / Initial Outlay

4. Payback Period vs Discounted Payback Period

Bharat AutoParts Robotic Line: Outlay = ₹80 Lakh, Cash Inflow = ₹25 Lakh/year for 5 years. Cost of capital = 12%.

Simple Payback Period

$\text{Payback} = \frac{\text{Outlay}}{\text{Annual Inflow}} = \frac{₹80\text{L}}{₹25\text{L}} = \mathbf{3.2\text{ years}}$.

• Completely ignores the time value of money.

Discounted Payback Period

Accounts for the time value of money by discounting each ₹25L inflow at 12%.

Cumulative discounted recovery takes 4.29 years.

Question 1 · NPV Decision Rule 1 Mark
An independent project requires an initial outlay of ₹50,00,000. The present value of all expected future cash inflows discounted at the company's cost of capital (10%) is ₹57,00,000. What is the project's Net Present Value (NPV), and what is the decision?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A
$\text{NPV} = \text{PV of Inflows} - \text{Initial Outlay} = ₹57,00,000 - ₹50,00,000 = +₹7,00,000$. Since $\text{NPV} > 0$, the project earns more than the required 10% hurdle rate and should be accepted.
Question 2 · IRR Definition 1 Mark
What is the definition of the Internal Rate of Return (IRR)?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The Internal Rate of Return (IRR) is by definition the exact hurdle/discount rate at which the present value of future cash inflows equals the initial investment outlay, resulting in $\text{NPV} = 0$.
Question 3 · The Big Conflict 1 Mark
When appraising two mutually exclusive capital investment projects, Project X has an NPV of ₹20,00,000 and an IRR of 16%. Project Y has an NPV of ₹6,00,000 and an IRR of 28%. The firm's cost of capital is 11%. Which project should management select?
Correct Answer: B (Select Project X)
Core Exam Doctrine: When mutually exclusive projects produce conflicting rankings, NPV always prevails. Project X adds ₹20 Lakh of direct wealth to shareholders, whereas Project Y adds only ₹6 Lakh. IRR fails because it is scale-blind and assumes unrealistic reinvestment at 28%.
Question 4 · Payback Calculation 1 Mark
A machinery automation project requires an upfront investment of ₹60,00,000 and generates constant cash inflows of ₹15,00,000 per year for 6 years. What is the simple payback period?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (4.0 years)
$\text{Payback Period} = \frac{\text{Initial Outlay}}{\text{Annual Cash Inflow}} = \frac{₹60,00,000}{₹15,00,000} = 4.0\text{ years}$.
Question 5 · Limitations of Payback 1 Mark
Which of the following is considered a primary theoretical flaw of the conventional simple Payback Period method?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The conventional payback period fails on two critical grounds: (1) It weights a rupee received in Year 3 identically to a rupee received in Year 1 (ignores TVM), and (2) it completely ignores massive cash inflows that may occur after the initial outlay is recovered.
Question 6 · Profitability Index 1 Mark
A project has an initial investment outlay of ₹80,00,000. The present value of its future cash inflows discounted at 12% is ₹90,40,000. What is the Profitability Index (PI)?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (1.13)
$\text{PI} = \frac{\text{PV of Cash Inflows}}{\text{Initial Outlay}} = \frac{₹90,40,000}{₹80,00,000} = 1.13$.
Since $\text{PI} > 1.0$, the project is acceptable and generates positive NPV (+₹10.4 Lakh).
Question 7 · Capital Rationing 1 Mark
Under conditions of strict capital rationing (limited capital budget), which appraisal method is recommended to rank divisible independent investment proposals?
Correct Answer: B (Profitability Index)
Explanation: When the total capital budget is capped, ranking by Profitability Index ($\text{PI} = \frac{\text{PV of Inflows}}{\text{Outlay}}$) maximizes the bang-for-buck (NPV generated per rupee of constrained budget).
Question 8 · Reinvestment Rate Assumption 1 Mark
What reinvestment rate does the Net Present Value (NPV) method assume for intermediate cash inflows?
Correct Answer: B (The firm's Cost of Capital)
Explanation: NPV assumes that intermediate cash flows can be reinvested at the project's opportunity cost of capital (WACC), which is economically realistic. In contrast, IRR assumes reinvestment at the IRR rate itself, which is often overly optimistic.
Question 9 · Additive Property 1 Mark
If a company undertakes two independent projects where Project 1 has an NPV of +₹15 Lakh and Project 2 has an NPV of +₹25 Lakh, what is the combined value added to the company?
Correct Answer: B (₹40 Lakh)
Explanation: One of the key advantages of NPV is its value-additivity property: $\text{NPV}(A + B) = \text{NPV}(A) + \text{NPV}(B)$. Rates of return (IRR) cannot be simply added together!
Question 10 · Cash Flow Forecasting Pitfall 1 Mark
When forecasting cash flows for capital budgeting analysis, which of the following items must be strictly **EXCLUDED**?
Correct Answer: C (Sunk costs)
Explanation: Capital budgeting evaluates only incremental future cash flows. Money already spent on prior market research or consultants (sunk costs) cannot be altered by accepting or rejecting the project and must be excluded.
