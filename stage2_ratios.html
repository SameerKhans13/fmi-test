Stage 2: Financial Ratio Analysis & DuPont Decomposition

Formulas will be provided in the exam hall, but interpretation is the key differentiator. Master liquidity, working capital cycles, leverage risk, and the 3-part DuPont breakdown.

1. The Five Ratio Families & Key Formulas

Ratios provide comparative insight into a company's financial health. Always compare over time, against industry peers, and against management budgets.

FamilyCore Question AnsweredPrimary Ratios
1. Liquidity Can the firm pay its short-term debts within the year? Current Ratio, Quick Ratio, Cash Ratio
2. Efficiency / Working Capital How swiftly does the company turn inventory and sales into cash? Inventory Days, Receivable Days, Payable Days, Operating Cycle
3. Solvency / Leverage How risky is long-term debt, and can earnings cover interest? Debt-to-Equity (D/E), Interest Coverage Ratio
4. Profitability & Returns How effectively does the business convert sales and capital into profit? Gross Margin, Operating Margin, Net Margin, ROCE, ROE, ROA
5. Market / Valuation What price is the market paying for each rupee of earnings or assets? P/E, Dividend Yield, Dividend Payout, Market Cap, Enterprise Value (EV)

2. Liquidity & The Working Capital Operating Cycle

Current Ratio
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities

Benchmark: 1.5× to 2.0×. Measures general short-term cushion.

Quick / Acid-Test Ratio
Quick Ratio = (Current Assets − Inventory) / Current Liabilities

Excludes inventory because inventory cannot be converted to cash instantly without discounts.

The Operating (Cash Conversion) Cycle Equation:
Operating Cycle = Inventory Days + Receivable Days − Payable Days

💡 Understanding Operating Cycle Dynamics

• Inventory Days ((365 × Inventory) / COGS): How long raw materials and goods sit in the warehouse before being sold.

• Receivable Days ((365 × Debtors) / Sales): How long customers take to pay cash after receiving the invoice.

• Payable Days ((365 × Creditors) / COGS): How long our business takes to pay trade suppliers.

• Interpretation: If Inventory Days = 50, Receivable Days = 40, and Payable Days = 30, the company waits 50 + 40 − 30 = 60 days between paying suppliers and collecting cash. This 60-day gap must be funded by bank working capital loans!

3. Solvency & Debt Risk: D/E and Interest Coverage

Debt-to-Equity (D/E) Ratio
D/E = Total Interest-Bearing Debt / Shareholders' Equity
Interest Coverage Ratio (Times Interest Earned)
Interest Coverage = EBIT / Interest Expense

⚠️ Critical Instructor Rules on Solvency:

• Interest Coverage below 2.0× is an immediate danger signal indicating that any minor drop in operating earnings could cause loan default.

• A company with low debt (e.g., Asian Paints at 0.06×) is "lightly geared," meaning high safety and room to borrow if needed.

4. DuPont Analysis: Decomposing Return on Equity (ROE)

Return on Equity (PAT / Equity) tells you how much profit shareholders get. However, two companies can have the same 15% ROE for completely different reasons. DuPont breaks ROE into three multiplying components:

The 3-Step DuPont Identity:
ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
PAT / Equity = (PAT / Sales) × (Sales / Assets) × (Assets / Equity)
DuPont ComponentFormulaWhat it MeasuresStrategic Archetype
1. Net Profit Margin PAT / Sales Pricing power & cost efficiency Luxury goods, high-end pharma (e.g., 20% margin, low turnover)
2. Asset Turnover Sales / Total Assets Volume velocity & asset productivity Discount retail supermarkets, discount airlines (e.g., 3% margin, 4.0× turnover)
3. Equity Multiplier Total Assets / Equity Financial leverage (1 + D/E) Banks and utilities (using borrowed money to boost ROE, but adds bankruptcy risk)

Question 1 · Liquidity 1 Mark
A firm has Current Assets of ₹80,00,000, Closing Inventory of ₹30,00,000, and Current Liabilities of ₹50,00,000. What are its Current Ratio and Quick Ratio?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A
Current Ratio = ₹80L / ₹50L = 1.60×.
Quick Ratio = (₹80L − ₹30L) / ₹50L = ₹50L / ₹50L = 1.00×.
Question 2 · Working Capital Cycle 1 Mark
A manufacturing unit reports the following working capital turnover days:
• Inventory holding period: 65 days
• Trade Receivables collection period: 45 days
• Trade Payables settlement period: 35 days
What is the duration of its Operating (Cash Conversion) Cycle?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (75 days)
Operating Cycle = Inventory Days + Receivable Days − Payable Days
= 65 + 45 − 35 = 110 − 35 = 75 days.
Question 3 · Solvency 1 Mark
Company Z has an EBIT of ₹12,00,000 and an annual Interest Expense of ₹8,00,000. How would a financial credit analyst interpret this interest coverage ratio?
Correct Answer: A
Interest Coverage = EBIT / Interest = ₹12L / ₹8L = 1.5×. The instructor highlighted that an interest coverage below 2.0× is an alarming warning sign that leaves minimal cushion if revenues decline.
Question 4 · DuPont Analysis 1 Mark
According to the DuPont 3-part identity, if two companies both report an identical Return on Equity (ROE) of 18%, but Company A has an Asset Turnover of 3.0× and an Equity Multiplier of 1.2×, while Company B has an Asset Turnover of 0.8× and an Equity Multiplier of 3.0×, what is true?
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: The Equity Multiplier is Assets / Equity = 1 + (Debt / Equity). Company B's multiplier of 3.0× means two-thirds of its assets are funded by debt (high financial risk), whereas Company A's multiplier of 1.2× reflects minimal debt and earns its ROE via rapid asset turnover (3.0×).
Question 5 · Efficiency Days 1 Mark
A retailer reports Net Annual Sales of ₹36,50,000 and Year-End Sundry Debtors of ₹4,00,000. Assuming a 365-day year, what is the average collection period (Receivable Days)?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: C (40 days)
Receivables Turnover = Sales / Debtors = ₹36,50,000 / ₹4,00,000 = 9.125×.
Collection Days = (365 × ₹4,00,000) / ₹36,50,000 = (365 / 36,50,000) × 4,00,000 = (1 / 10,000) × 4,00,000 = 40 days.
Question 6 · Market Valuation 1 Mark
A company has 20,00,000 equity shares trading at a market price of ₹150 per share. It has total interest-bearing debt of ₹8,00,00,000 and cash reserves of ₹2,00,00,000. What is its Enterprise Value (EV)?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (₹36 Crore)
1. Market Capitalisation = 20L shares × ₹150 = ₹30,00,00,000 (₹30 Crore).
2. Enterprise Value = Market Cap + Debt − Cash
= ₹30 Cr + ₹8 Cr − ₹2 Cr = ₹36 Crore.
Question 7 · Interpretation 1 Mark
If a company's Inventory Days increase from 40 days to 85 days while sales remain constant, what is the immediate effect on its cash balance and working capital?
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: When inventory sits longer before being sold, extra cash is tied up in the warehouse. This directly lengthens the Operating Cycle (Inventory Days + Receivable Days − Payable Days) and creates a cash drain.
Question 8 · Profitability 1 Mark
Gamma Ltd has Sales of ₹50,00,000, Cost of Goods Sold of ₹30,00,000, Operating Expenses of ₹10,00,000, and Net Profit after Tax (PAT) of ₹6,00,000. What are its Gross Margin and Operating Margin?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A
Gross Profit = ₹50L − ₹30L = ₹20L → Gross Margin = ₹20L / ₹50L = 40%.
Operating Profit (EBIT) = Gross Profit − Opex = ₹20L − ₹10L = ₹10L → Operating Margin = ₹10L / ₹50L = 20%.
Question 9 · Valuation Ratios 1 Mark
A firm reports Net Profit (PAT) of ₹40,00,000 with 10,00,000 equity shares outstanding. The current stock price is ₹100, and it declares a Dividend per Share (DPS) of ₹2.00. What are its P/E Ratio and Dividend Payout Ratio?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (P/E = 25× | Payout = 50%)
1. EPS = ₹40,00,000 / 10,00,000 shares = ₹4.00 per share.
2. P/E Ratio = Market Price / EPS = ₹100 / ₹4.00 = 25.0×.
3. Dividend Payout = DPS / EPS = ₹2.00 / ₹4.00 = 50%.
Question 10 · Asian Paints Case 1 Mark
In the Asian Paints standalone case discussed in class, the company has a Debt-to-Equity ratio of 0.06× and an Interest Coverage ratio of 35.1×. What does this reveal about its capital structure?
Correct Answer: B
