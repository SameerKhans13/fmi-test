1. The Accounting Equation: Who Owns What and Who Funded It

Accounting records, classifies, and summarises business transactions in monetary terms. Every single transaction preserves the fundamental equality:

Fundamental Balance Sheet Equation:
Assets = Liabilities + Equity
(For a company: Shareholders' Equity; for a sole proprietorship: Owner's Equity)

The Seven Accounting Elements: Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Income, Expenses, Gains, Losses.

💡 Equity Mechanics

• Income and Gains increase Equity.

• Expenses and Losses reduce Equity.

• Drawings (Proprietorship) / Dividends (Company) reduce Equity directly. They are NOT expenses; they are a withdrawal of capital by the owners.

• Equation Movement: ΔEquity = Income − Expenses + Gains − Losses − Drawings/Dividends

2. Core Assumptions & Accounting Conventions (High Exam Weightage)

The exam frequently tests your ability to identify the correct convention applied in a scenario:

Assumption / Convention What it Dictates Real-Life Example from Slides
Business Entity The business is legally and financially distinct from its owners. Amazon’s corporate books do not include Jeff Bezos’s personal real estate or private jets.
Going Concern The business is assumed to operate indefinitely. Assets are valued at historical cost less wear, not forced liquidation value. A bakery values its ₹15L commercial oven at depreciated book value, not at scrap/distress sale price.
Money Measurement Only events that can be expressed reliably in monetary terms are recorded. Employee morale, top-tier engineering talent, or brand reputation cannot be an asset line on the balance sheet.
Accounting Period The infinite life of a business is split into standard equal periods for reporting. Indian financial year: 1st April to 31st March.
Accrual Concept Revenues and expenses are recognised when the economic event occurs, regardless of when cash moves. March electricity bill of ₹40,000 paid on 15th April is recorded as a March expense with a liability (expenses payable).
Matching Principle Expenses incurred to generate a specific revenue must be paired in the same period as that revenue. Buying 1,000 shirts for ₹10L and selling 600 shirts means COGS is only ₹6L. The remaining ₹4L is an asset (unsold closing stock), not an expense.
Realisation Principle Revenue is recognised only when performance obligations are fulfilled, not upon cash receipt. ₹12,000 upfront annual Netflix subscription paid on 1st April is recognised at ₹1,000/month; the unearned portion is a liability.
Prudence (Conservatism) Anticipate no profits, but provide for all possible losses. Do not overstate assets or understate liabilities. Valuing inventory at Lower of Cost or Net Realisable Value (NRV); creating a provision for doubtful debts.
Materiality Financial reporting focuses on items significant enough to alter user decisions. Trivial items are expensed immediately. A ₹500 office stapler with a 10-year physical life is expensed as stationery, not capitalized as a fixed asset.

3. The Three Statements & Net Income vs Operating Cash Flow

Why Net Income ≠ Operating Cash Flow: Profit under accrual accounting reflects economic performance, while cash flow reflects actual liquidity.

Indirect Method: Reconciling PAT to Operating Cash Flow (CFO)
CFO = PAT + Depreciation − ΔCurrent Assets + ΔCurrent Liabilities

⚠️ Golden Rules for Working Capital Adjustments:

• Increase in Receivables (Debtors) → SUBTRACT (Cash tied up in credit sales; revenue recorded but cash not received).

• Increase in Inventory → SUBTRACT (Cash consumed to purchase extra stock).

• Increase in Payables (Creditors) → ADD (Suppliers financed our purchases; cash preserved).

• Decrease in Receivables/Inventory → ADD (Cash collected from customers / stock liquidated).

4. Depreciation Methods: Straight-Line vs Reducing-Balance

Depreciation is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of a non-current tangible asset over its estimated useful life.

Straight-Line Method (SLM)

Formula: Annual Depreciation = (Cost − Residual Value) / Useful Life

• Charge is identical each year.

• Example: Machine cost ₹11L, residual value ₹1L, life 5 years → (₹11L − ₹1L) / 5 = ₹2L / year.

Reducing-Balance Method (WDV)

Formula: Depreciation = Opening Book Value × Depreciation Rate r

• Charge is highest in Year 1 and steadily decreases.

• Best suits assets that lose value rapidly (vehicles, technology).

Question 1 · Theory 1 Mark
A manufacturing company buys a plant for ₹40,00,000. Due to a sudden market crash, identical second-hand plants are selling for ₹10,00,000. The accountant continues to report the asset at cost less accumulated depreciation rather than marking it down to scrap value. Which accounting principle supports this?
Correct Answer: B (Going Concern Assumption)
Explanation: The Going Concern assumption presumes that the business will continue operations for the foreseeable future. Hence, assets are recorded at historical cost less depreciation (reflecting productive capacity) rather than immediate liquidation/scrap value.
Question 2 · Arithmetic 1 Mark
Amit starts a trading business on 1st January with ₹8,00,000 equity cash. During the month:
• He takes a bank loan of ₹4,00,000 cash.
• He buys inventory for ₹5,00,000 cash.
• He sells half the inventory (cost ₹2,50,000) for ₹4,20,000 cash.
• He withdraws ₹50,000 cash for his personal household expenses.
What are the Total Assets and Equity at the end of the month?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (Assets = ₹13,20,000 | Equity = ₹9,20,000)
Step-by-step arithmetic:
1. Cash inflows: Initial Equity ₹8.0L + Bank loan ₹4.0L + Sales ₹4.2L = ₹16.2L.
2. Cash outflows: Inventory bought ₹5.0L + Drawings ₹0.5L = ₹5.5L.
3. Final Cash balance = ₹16.2L − ₹5.5L = ₹10.7L.
4. Remaining Inventory = ₹5.0L − ₹2.5L = ₹2.5L.
5. Total Assets = Cash ₹10.7L + Inventory ₹2.5L = ₹13.20 Lakh.
6. Total Liabilities = Bank Loan = ₹4.0 Lakh.
7. Equity = Initial ₹8.0L + Profit (₹4.2L − ₹2.5L = ₹1.7L) − Drawings ₹0.5L = ₹9.20 Lakh.
Balance check: Assets ₹13.20L = Liabilities ₹4.0L + Equity ₹9.20L.
Question 3 · Theory 1 Mark
Under the matching concept, how should the cost of unsold merchandise inventory remaining at the end of the accounting period be treated?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The matching principle dictates that costs must be paired with the revenues they produce. Because unsold goods produced zero revenue in the current period, they cannot be expensed; they must be carried forward as a Current Asset (Closing Inventory) to future periods.
Question 4 · Revenue Realisation 1 Mark
On 1st October 2025, an educational coaching institute collects ₹1,20,000 cash as full annual tuition fee for a 12-month program ending 30th September 2026. For the financial year ending 31st March 2026, what revenue and balance sheet item should be reported?
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: From 1st October 2025 to 31st March 2026, exactly 6 months of teaching service has been rendered: (6 / 12) × ₹1,20,000 = ₹60,000 earned revenue. The remaining 6 months (₹60,000) represents an obligation to perform future services and must be shown as a Current Liability (Unearned / Deferred Revenue).
Question 5 · Cash Flow Linkage 1 Mark
A corporate entity reports Net Profit after Tax (PAT) of ₹45,00,000 for FY2026. The financial records show:
• Depreciation on assets: ₹10,00,000
• Accounts Receivable increased by ₹8,00,000
• Inventory increased by ₹5,00,000
• Accounts Payable increased by ₹4,00,000
What is the Cash Flow from Operating Activities?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (₹46,00,000)
Manual calculation:
CFO = PAT + Depreciation − ΔReceivables − ΔInventory + ΔPayables

= ₹45,00,000 + ₹10,00,000 − ₹8,00,000 − ₹5,00,000 + ₹4,00,000

= ₹55,00,000 − ₹13,00,000 + ₹4,00,000 = ₹46,00,000

Question 6 · Conventions 1 Mark
Why does an accountant immediately write off an executive desk calculator costing ₹400 as an administrative expense in Year 1, even though it will be used for 8 years?
Correct Answer: B (Materiality Convention)
Explanation: The Materiality convention permits accountants to disregard strict theoretical accounting treatments for items whose value is too insignificant to influence the economic decisions of users. Depreciating ₹50 a year for 8 years would create administrative burden without adding value to decision-makers.
Question 7 · Depreciation 1 Mark
A delivery vehicle is purchased for ₹12,00,000. It has an estimated life of 5 years and a salvage value of ₹2,00,000. Under the Straight-Line Method (SLM), what is the book value at the end of Year 3?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (₹6,00,000)
Step-by-step arithmetic:
1. Depreciable Amount = Cost − Salvage = ₹12,00,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹10,00,000.
2. Annual Depreciation = ₹10,00,000 / 5 = ₹2,00,000 / year.
3. Accumulated Depreciation after 3 years = 3 × ₹2,00,000 = ₹6,00,000.
4. Book Value at end of Year 3 = Cost − Accumulated Depreciation = ₹12,00,000 − ₹6,00,000 = ₹6,00,000.
Question 8 · Conceptual Linkage 1 Mark
Which of the following cash transactions alters both the Balance Sheet and Owner's Equity, but has ZERO impact on the Income Statement?
Correct Answer: B (Owner drawings)
Explanation: Drawings / dividends are distributions of wealth to the owners. They reduce Cash (Assets) and directly reduce Equity on the Balance Sheet. They are NOT operating expenses and therefore do not appear on the Income Statement. (Options A, C, and D are all expenses that flow through the Income Statement).

📑 Quick Formula Reference

• Accounting Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity

• CFO (Indirect): PAT + Depreciation − ΔCA + ΔCL

• SLM Depreciation: (Cost − Salvage) / Useful Life
