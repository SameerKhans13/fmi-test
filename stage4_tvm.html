1. Why a Rupee Today is Worth More Than a Rupee Later

A rational individual prefers receiving money today rather than in the future due to four fundamental economic reasons:

1. Present Consumption

People prefer consuming goods today rather than waiting for tomorrow.

2. Productive Investment

₹1 today can be invested at interest rate $r$ to earn a return and multiply.

3. Inflation Erosion

Rising prices erode the purchasing power of money over time.

4. Default / Credit Risk

A future promised payment always carries a risk of non-payment.

2. Compounding, Discounting & The Rule of 72

Compounding (Moving Forward to FV)
FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ

• Example: ₹10,000 at 10% for 3 years:
Yr 1: ₹11,000 $\rightarrow$ Yr 2: ₹12,100 $\rightarrow$ Yr 3: ₹13,310.

Discounting (Pulling Back to PV)
PV = FV / (1 + r)ⁿ = FV × (1 + r)⁻ⁿ

• Example: ₹13,310 due in 3 years at 10% is worth exactly ₹10,000 today.

⚡ The Rule of 72 (Mental Math Shortcut)
Years to Double ≈ 72 / Interest Rate (%)

• At 8% interest: Money doubles in approximately $\frac{72}{8} = \mathbf{9\text{ years}}$.
• At 12% interest: Money doubles in approximately $\frac{72}{12} = \mathbf{6\text{ years}}$.
• At 6% interest: Money doubles in approximately $\frac{72}{6} = \mathbf{12\text{ years}}$.

💡 Compounding Frequency Insight (APR vs Nominal):

If a credit card charges 2% per month, the annual cost is NOT $2\% \times 12 = 24\%$. Because interest compounds monthly, ₹100 becomes $(1.02)^{12} = ₹126.82$, representing an Annual Percentage Rate (APR) of 26.82%!

3. Annuities & Perpetuities

Cash Flow StructureFormula for Present Value (PV)Typical Real-World Application
Ordinary Annuity
(Equal payments at end of each period)		 $PV = PMT \times \left[\frac{1 - (1+r)^{-n}}{r}\right]$ Bank loans, EMIs, bond semi-annual coupon payments.
Annuity Due
(Equal payments at start of each period)		 $PV_{\text{due}} = PV_{\text{ordinary}} \times (1 + r)$ Apartment rent, commercial equipment leases, insurance premiums.
Level Perpetuity
(Constant payment forever)		 $PV = \frac{PMT}{r}$ Perpetual preference shares, permanent university endowments.
Growing Perpetuity
(Grows at constant rate $g$ forever, $r > g$)		 $PV = \frac{CF_1}{r - g}$ Gordon Growth equity share valuation model.

⚠️ Ordinary Annuity vs Annuity Due Comparison:

Because every single cash flow in an Annuity Due is received one period earlier, each payment earns an extra period of interest. Therefore: $$\mathbf{PV(\text{Annuity Due}) = PV(\text{Ordinary Annuity}) \times (1 + r)}$$ Worked Example: A 4-year lease of ₹2,00,000/year at 9%: • Ordinary Annuity PV (paid at year-end) = $₹2,00,000 \times 3.2397 = ₹6,47,944$. • Annuity Due PV (paid at start of each year) = $₹6,47,944 \times 1.09 = \mathbf{₹7,06,259}$.

4. Slide Case Walkthroughs: GreenCart & Patel Industries

Case A: GreenCart's Investor Choice

An angel investor offers GreenCart: ₹8,00,000 today OR ₹10,50,000 in 3 years. GreenCart can earn 12% annual return on funds. Which offer should GreenCart choose?

• Method 1 (Discounting to today): $PV = \frac{₹10,50,000}{(1.12)^3} = \frac{₹10,50,000}{1.4049} = \mathbf{₹7,47,369}$.

• Since ₹8,00,000 today is greater than the discounted value of ₹7,47,369, GreenCart should take ₹8,00,000 today!

• Method 2 (Compounding forward): $₹8,00,000 \times (1.12)^3 = \mathbf{₹11,23,942}$ in 3 years, which easily beats ₹10,50,000.

Case B: Perpetuity Comparison

A donor offers an educational trust ₹10,000 every year forever. If the discount rate is 8%:

• $PV = \frac{₹10,000}{0.08} = \mathbf{₹1,25,000}$ today.

• If offered a choice between ₹1,00,000 now OR ₹1,20,000 in 3 years at 8%: $PV = \frac{₹1,20,000}{(1.08)^3} = \frac{₹1,20,000}{1.2597} = \mathbf{₹95,260}$. Taking ₹1,00,000 now is superior!

Question 1 · Rule of 72 1 Mark
According to the Rule of 72, if an investor deposits ₹50,000 in a fixed deposit earning an annual interest rate of 6% compounded annually, approximately how many years will it take for the money to grow to ₹1,00,000?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: C (12 years)
$\text{Years to double} \approx \frac{72}{\text{Interest Rate \%}} = \frac{72}{6} = 12\text{ years}$.
Question 2 · Compounding 1 Mark
A sum of ₹10,000 is invested today at an annual interest rate of 10% compounded annually. What is its Future Value (FV) at the end of 3 years?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (₹13,310)
$FV = ₹10,000 \times (1 + 0.10)^3 = ₹10,000 \times 1.331 = ₹13,310$.
(Year 1: ₹11,000 $\rightarrow$ Year 2: ₹12,100 $\rightarrow$ Year 3: ₹13,310). Note that simple interest would give only ₹13,000.
Question 3 · Investment Choice 1 Mark
You are offered an upfront payment of ₹1,00,000 today OR a payment of ₹1,20,000 in 3 years. If the applicable market discount rate is 8% per annum (where $1.08^3 \approx 1.2597$), which option should you choose?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B
Discounting the future sum to today:
$PV = \frac{₹1,20,000}{(1.08)^3} = \frac{₹1,20,000}{1.2597} \approx ₹95,260$.
Since ₹1,00,000 in hand today exceeds ₹95,260, taking ₹1,00,000 today is the financially superior choice!
Question 4 · Level Perpetuity 1 Mark
An alumnus wishes to establish a scholarship endowment that will pay ₹4,00,000 every year forever. If the fund can earn a guaranteed annual return of 8%, what lump sum must be deposited into the endowment today?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (₹50,00,000)
$\text{PV of Level Perpetuity} = \frac{PMT}{r} = \frac{₹4,00,000}{0.08} = \frac{4,00,000}{8/100} = \frac{40,000,000}{8} = ₹50,00,000$.
Question 5 · Growing Perpetuity 1 Mark
A real estate asset produces net rental cash flow of ₹6,00,000 next year. Rentals are expected to grow at a constant rate of 4% per year indefinitely. If the required rate of return is 10%, what is the present value of this asset?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (₹1 Crore)
$\text{PV of Growing Perpetuity} = \frac{CF_1}{r - g} = \frac{₹6,00,000}{0.10 - 0.04} = \frac{₹6,00,000}{0.06} = ₹1,00,00,000\text{ (₹1 Crore)}$.
(Notice that growth significantly increases value: without growth, ₹6L at 10% would be worth only ₹60 Lakh).
Question 6 · Annuity Due vs Ordinary 1 Mark
If an Ordinary Annuity of ₹1,00,000 per year for 4 years at a discount rate of 10% has a Present Value of ₹3,16,986, what is the Present Value of an otherwise identical **Annuity Due** where payments occur at the beginning of each year?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (₹3,48,685)
An Annuity Due has payments made at the start of each period, meaning every cash flow is discounted by one fewer year. Mathematically:
$PV_{\text{Due}} = PV_{\text{Ordinary}} \times (1 + r) = ₹3,16,986 \times 1.10 = ₹3,48,685$.
Question 7 · Uneven Cash Flows 1 Mark
An investment promises cash flows of ₹11,000 at the end of Year 1 and ₹24,200 at the end of Year 2. If the discount rate is 10%, what is the total Present Value?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (₹30,000)
Discount each flow by its respective year:
• Year 1 PV = $\frac{₹11,000}{1.10} = ₹10,000$.
• Year 2 PV = $\frac{₹24,200}{(1.10)^2} = \frac{₹24,200}{1.21} = ₹20,000$.
• Total PV = $₹10,000 + ₹20,000 = ₹30,000$.
Question 8 · Discount Rate Sensitivity 1 Mark
What is the relationship between the discount rate and the present value of a future cash flow?
Correct Answer: B (Inverse relationship)
Explanation: In the formula $PV = \frac{FV}{(1+r)^n}$, the discount rate $r$ sits in the denominator. A higher required rate of return or discount rate shrinks the present value of distant cash flows.
