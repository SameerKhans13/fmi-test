Stage 3: Management Accounting & Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP)

The instructor explicitly warned: CVP and Break-Even formulas will NOT be on the formula sheet! You must know them by heart, along with cost behaviors, sunk costs, and sensitivity analysis.

⚡ MUST-MEMORIZE CVP FORMULAS (Exam Hall Essential) Not on Formula Sheet

Commit these exact equations to memory before test day:

1. Unit Contribution
Contribution = SP − VC
Selling Price minus Variable Cost per unit
2. Profit-Volume (P/V) Ratio
P/V = (SP − VC) / SP = Contribution / Sales
Also called Contribution Margin Ratio (%)
3. Break-Even Point in Units
BEP (Units) = Fixed Cost / Unit Contribution
Volume where Total Revenue = Total Cost (Profit = 0)
4. Break-Even Point in Value (₹)
BEP (₹) = Fixed Cost / P/V Ratio
Rupee sales needed to cover all fixed costs
5. Target Profit Sales (Units)
Required Units = (Fixed Cost + Target Profit) / Unit Contribution
Units required to achieve a specific target profit
6. Margin of Safety (MoS)
MoS = Actual Sales − Break-Even Sales
MoS (₹) = Profit / P/V Ratio
Cushion before the business starts incurring losses

2. Cost Classifications for Decision Making

DimensionClassificationMeaning & Real-Life Example
Traceability Direct Cost (Prime) Physically traced to the final cost object. (e.g., Fabric for a shirt, steel for an auto body).
Indirect Cost (Overhead) Cannot be economically traced directly; shared among many units. (e.g., Factory rent, security guard salary).
Cost Behaviour Fixed Cost Constant in total irrespective of output, but falls per unit as volume expands. (e.g., Factory lease ₹10L/month).
Variable Cost Constant per unit, but total varies directly in proportion to production volume. (e.g., Raw material ₹20/bottle).
Semi-Variable Cost Contains both a fixed baseline charge and a variable per-unit component. (e.g., Electricity bill: ₹50,000 fixed + ₹8/machine hour).
Decision Relevance Relevant Cost Future costs that differ among decision alternatives. (e.g., Extra material needed only if special order is accepted).
Sunk Cost (IGNORE!) Historical cost already incurred and irreversible. Irrelevant to any future choice! (e.g., ₹2 Crore already spent building software).
Opportunity Cost The monetary benefit sacrificed from the next best alternative forgone. (e.g., Foregone ₹16L job salary to pursue an MBA).

3. Absorption Costing vs Marginal Costing: The Profit Gap

A classic exam question asks why profit differs between Absorption and Marginal costing when production $\neq$ sales.

BasisAbsorption Costing (GAAP)Marginal Costing (Managerial)
Product Unit Cost Includes: Direct Material + Direct Labour + Variable Overhead + Allocated Fixed Overhead Direct Material + Direct Labour + Variable Overhead (Variable costs ONLY)
Treatment of Fixed Factory Overhead: Absorbed into units produced. Unsold units carry fixed overheads into closing stock. Treated as a Period Cost and written off in full against the period's contribution.
When Production > Sales (Inventory Rises): Higher profit (part of fixed cost is stored inside unsold inventory on the balance sheet). Lower profit (all fixed overheads were expensed immediately).
When Sales > Production (Inventory Depleted): Lower profit (prior period fixed costs stored in inventory are now released as COGS). Higher profit.

💡 The Reconciliation Formula:

$\text{Absorption Profit} - \text{Marginal Profit} = (\text{Closing Inventory Units} - \text{Opening Inventory Units}) \times \text{Fixed Overhead Absorption Rate per Unit}$.

Question 1 · Break-Even Units 1 Mark
A small manufacturing company sells customized water bottles for ₹500 each. The variable cost per bottle is ₹300, and annual fixed operating overheads total ₹10,00,000. How many bottles must the company sell each year to achieve break-even?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: C (5,000 bottles)
$\text{Unit Contribution} = \text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost} = ₹500 - ₹300 = ₹200$.
$\text{Break-Even Units} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{Unit Contribution}} = \frac{₹10,00,000}{₹200} = 5,000\text{ units}$.
Question 2 · P/V Ratio & Break-Even Value 1 Mark
A boutique consulting firm charges ₹2,500 per billable hour. Direct variable costs are ₹1,500 per hour, and monthly fixed office overheads are ₹6,00,000. What is the firm's Profit-Volume (P/V) Ratio and its Break-Even Sales Value per month?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A
1. $\text{Unit Contribution} = ₹2,500 - ₹1,500 = ₹1,000$.
2. $\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{₹1,000}{₹2,500} = \frac{2}{5} = \mathbf{40\%}$.
3. $\text{Break-Even Value} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} = \frac{₹6,00,000}{0.40} = ₹15,00,000$.
Question 3 · Target Profit 1 Mark
Using the data from Question 1 (SP = ₹500, VC = ₹300, FC = ₹10,00,000), how many units must the company sell if management targets an annual operating profit of ₹4,00,000?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (7,000 units)
$\text{Required Units} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost} + \text{Target Profit}}{\text{Unit Contribution}} = \frac{₹10,00,000 + ₹4,00,000}{₹200} = \frac{₹14,00,000}{₹200} = 7,000\text{ units}$.
Question 4 · Margin of Safety 1 Mark
A cloud kitchen sells meals for ₹250 each. Variable costs are ₹160 per meal, and monthly fixed costs are ₹6,00,000. If current monthly sales are 9,000 meals (revenue ₹22.5 Lakh), what is the Margin of Safety in percentage terms?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (25.9%)
1. $\text{Unit Contribution} = ₹250 - ₹160 = ₹90$.
2. $\text{Break-Even Units} = \frac{₹6,00,000}{₹90} \approx 6,667\text{ meals}$.
3. $\text{Margin of Safety (Units)} = 9,000 - 6,667 = 2,333\text{ meals}$.
4. $\text{MoS \%} = \frac{2,333}{9,000} \approx \mathbf{25.9\%}$.
Question 5 · Relevance & Sunk Costs 1 Mark
A software startup has already spent ₹50,00,000 developing a mobile app. To launch the app, an additional ₹10,00,000 marketing campaign is required, which will bring in ₹15,00,000 of immediate subscription revenue. Which statement reflects proper managerial decision analysis?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Sunk costs have already been spent and cannot be recovered regardless of future decisions. In managerial economics, decision makers look strictly at future incremental cash flows: Extra Inflow (₹15L) − Extra Outflow (₹10L) = $+₹5\text{L}$ incremental cash profit! Launching is the correct choice.
Question 6 · Capacity & Opportunity Cost 1 Mark
A flight has 5 remaining empty seats. Normal passengers pay ₹8,000 per ticket (marginal cost of passenger service = ₹1,000). A corporate tour operator offers ₹3,000 per ticket for all 5 seats. If individual passengers are readily available to purchase these 5 seats at ₹7,000 each, what should the airline do?
Correct Answer: B
Slide worked example:
• Contribution from individual = $₹7,000 - ₹1,000 = ₹6,000$ per seat.
• Contribution from corporate = $₹3,000 - ₹1,000 = ₹2,000$ per seat.
• Because capacity is constrained (only 5 seats remain), the opportunity cost of selling to the tour firm is the ₹6,000 contribution foregone.
• Selling to the corporate firm causes a net loss of $5 \times (₹6,000 - ₹2,000) = \mathbf{₹20,000}$ of contribution! Reject the offer.
Question 7 · Price Sensitivity 1 Mark
Why does a selling price reduction have an unusually severe impact on a business's break-even point?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: In the break-even formula $\text{BEP} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{SP - VC}$, the denominator is unit contribution. A 10% price cut might wipe out 30–50% of the unit contribution, resulting in an enormous increase in the units required to break even.
Question 8 · Absorption vs Marginal Profit 1 Mark
During Year 1, Omega Ltd manufactures 10,000 units of product and sells 8,000 units. Fixed manufacturing overhead is ₹100 per unit. Opening inventory was zero. Which costing method will report higher profit, and by how much?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (Absorption higher by ₹2,00,000)
Slide 54 exact case:
Unsold units = $10,000\text{ produced} - 8,000\text{ sold} = 2,000\text{ units}$ in closing stock.
Under Absorption Costing, $2,000 \times ₹100 = ₹2,00,000$ of fixed overhead is capitalized into closing inventory on the balance sheet instead of being expensed in the current year. Under Marginal Costing, all fixed overhead is expensed immediately. Thus, Absorption profit is ₹2,00,000 higher.
Question 9 · Cost Behavior 1 Mark
As production volume increases within the relevant range, what happens to Fixed Cost per unit and Variable Cost per unit?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Total fixed cost is constant, so dividing it across more units causes fixed cost per unit to decline. Conversely, variable cost per unit remains constant (while total variable cost increases linearly with volume).
Question 10 · Margin of Safety in Value 1 Mark
A firm has actual annual sales revenue of ₹10,00,000, fixed overheads of ₹3,60,000, and a Profit-Volume (P/V) ratio of 40%. What is its Margin of Safety in rupees (value)?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: A (₹1,00,000)
1. $\text{Break-Even Sales Value} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} = \frac{₹3,60,000}{0.40} = ₹9,00,000$.
2. $\text{Margin of Safety (Value)} = \text{Actual Sales} - \text{Break-Even Sales} = ₹10,00,000 - ₹9,00,000 = ₹1,00,000$.
(Shortcut verification: Total Contribution = $40\% \times ₹10\text{L} = ₹4\text{L}$. Profit = $₹4\text{L} - ₹3.6\text{L} = ₹40,000$. $\text{MoS} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{P/V}} = \frac{₹40,000}{0.40} = ₹1,00,000$).
