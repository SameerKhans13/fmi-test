1. Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) & Security Market Line

The CAPM calculates the expected required return on an equity security given its systematic (non-diversifiable) risk:

CAPM Cost of Equity Formula:
E(Rᵢ) = R_f + βᵢ × [E(Rₘ) − R_f]
ComponentWhat it RepresentsStandard Indian Market Input
Risk-Free Rate ($R_f$) Return on an asset with zero default and reinvestment risk. Yield on 10-year Government of India Security (G-Sec) ~7.0%.
Market Risk Premium ($R_m - R_f$) Extra return investors demand for holding equities over safe debt. Common historical range: 5.0% to 7.0%.
Beta ($\beta$) Sensitivity of the stock's returns relative to overall market swings. $\beta = 1.0$ (Market average); $\beta > 1$ (Aggressive); $\beta < 1$ (Defensive).

💡 Jensen's Alpha ($\alpha$)

$\mathbf{\alpha = \text{Actual Return} - \text{CAPM Expected Return}}$.
• If a stock with $\beta = 1.2$ was expected to return $14\%$ based on CAPM but actually generated $17\%$, it generated a **positive alpha of $+3\%$** (true value created beyond pure market exposure).

2. Financial Derivatives: Core Concepts & Participants

A financial derivative is a contract whose payoff is derived from the value of an underlying asset (equities, commodities, currencies, interest rates).

⚠️ Derivatives Golden Principle:

Derivatives transfer risk from those who wish to avoid it to those willing to bear it; they do not create or destroy fundamental economic risk.

The Three Market Participants:

1. Hedgers

Participate in derivatives to eliminate or reduce price risk in their core business. (e.g., An airline locking in jet fuel price; an exporter selling USD forward).

2. Speculators

Voluntarily take on price risk to earn profits from directional market price movements. They provide vital liquidity to the market.

3. Arbitrageurs

Exploit temporary price discrepancies between two markets to lock in riskless profit without putting up capital. Their actions keep market prices fair.

4. Options: Asymmetric Rights and Payoff Profiles

Unlike futures or forwards which create an obligation on both parties, an option grants the buyer a right, but NOT an obligation.

Call Option (Right to BUY)

• Buyer is Bullish (expects stock price to rise).

• Maximum Loss: Limited strictly to the premium paid upfront.

• Profit Potential: Theoretically unlimited as stock rises.

Put Option (Right to SELL)

• Buyer is Bearish (expects price to drop) or hedging an equity portfolio.

• Maximum Loss: Limited strictly to the premium paid.

• Profit Potential: Substantial (until stock price drops to ₹0).

Option Moneyness Definitions:

Moneyness StateCall Option ConditionPut Option ConditionIntrinsic Value
In-the-Money (ITM) Spot Price ($S$) > Strike Price ($K$) Spot Price ($S$) < Strike Price ($K$) Positive intrinsic value if exercised immediately.
At-the-Money (ATM) Spot Price ($S$) = Strike Price ($K$) Spot Price ($S$) = Strike Price ($K$) Zero intrinsic value; pure time value.
Out-of-the-Money (OTM) Spot Price ($S$) < Strike Price ($K$) Spot Price ($S$) > Strike Price ($K$) Zero intrinsic value; exercising would produce a loss.

💡 Exercise Styles: American vs European

• European Option: Can only be exercised on the final expiration date (e.g., Nifty index options).

• American Option: Can be exercised on ANY trading day up to and including expiration date. Because of this added flexibility, an American option is worth at least as much as an otherwise identical European option.

Question 1 · CAPM Calculation 1 Mark
According to the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM), if the 10-year Government bond yield is 7%, the expected market return is 13%, and a company's stock has a Beta ($\beta$) of 1.2, what is the required cost of equity?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (14.2%)
$\text{Market Risk Premium} = R_m - R_f = 13\% - 7\% = 6\%$.
$E(R_e) = R_f + \beta \times (R_m - R_f) = 7\% + 1.2 \times 6\% = 7\% + 7.2\% = 14.2\%$.
Question 2 · Beta Interpretation 1 Mark
An FMCG company producing household essentials (soap, toothpaste) typically exhibits a Beta of around 0.6. What does this indicate to an investor?
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: A beta below 1.0 indicates a defensive stock whose business revenues and earnings are relatively insulated from macroeconomic swings. If the broad market rises or falls by 10%, a stock with $\beta = 0.6$ is expected to move by only ~6%.
Question 3 · Derivatives Role 1 Mark
Which statement best summarizes the fundamental macroeconomic function of financial derivatives?
Correct Answer: B
Slide 37 exact note: "Derivatives transfer risk; they do not create or destroy it." They allow risk-averse firms (hedgers) to shed price volatility to risk-seeking investors (speculators).
Question 4 · Forwards vs Futures 1 Mark
Why do exchange-traded Futures contracts carry virtually zero counterparty default risk compared to over-the-counter (OTC) Forward contracts?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The exchange clearinghouse acts as the central counterparty for all market participants. By demanding initial margin and settling gains and losses daily via mark-to-market (MTM), credit risk is mitigated and counterparty default is prevented.
Question 5 · Options Payoff Asymmetry 1 Mark
An investor buys a Call Option on a stock with a strike price of ₹1,000 for a premium of ₹50. If at expiration the stock price crashes to ₹700, what is the buyer's net profit or loss?
Scratchpad / Rough Math:
Correct Answer: B (Loss of ₹50)
Explanation: An option gives the buyer the right, not the obligation to buy. Since the spot price is ₹700, buying at the strike of ₹1,000 makes no sense, so the buyer allows the option to expire worthless. The buyer's maximum possible loss is strictly capped at the upfront premium paid: ₹50!
Question 6 · Option Moneyness 1 Mark
A trader holds a Put Option with a strike price of ₹500. The current underlying spot market price of the stock is ₹420. What is the moneyness status of this option?
Correct Answer: A (In-the-Money)
Explanation: A Put option is the right to SELL. If the market price is ₹420, having the contractual right to sell at ₹500 has immediate positive intrinsic value of $₹500 - ₹420 = ₹80$. Therefore, the put option is In-the-Money (ITM).
Question 7 · Portfolio Insurance 1 Mark
An institutional mutual fund owns 1,00,000 shares of an IT company currently trading at ₹1,500. The fund manager wants to protect the portfolio against a potential market crash without selling the shares. What derivative position achieves this goal?
Correct Answer: B (Buy Put options)
Slide 40 case: Buying Put options establishes a price floor (portfolio insurance). If the stock price plummets, the gain on the put option offsets the loss on the stock, while retaining full upside if the stock continues to climb.
Question 8 · Option Styles 1 Mark
What is the distinguishing difference between an American-style option and a European-style option?
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The terms American and European describe the exercise timing rules: American options offer early exercise flexibility at any point prior to expiration, whereas European options can only be exercised on the expiry date itself.
